2. Constants

Gas constantR = 0.08206 L·atm·mol−1·K−1 = 8.314 J·mol−1·K−1
Avogadro's numberNA = 6.022 × 1023 mol−1
TemperatureK = °C + 273.15
Pressure1 atm = 760 torr = 760 mmHg
Water heat data (typical exam sheet)Cm(ice)=37.6 J·mol−1·K−1; Cm(liquid)=75.2 J·mol−1·K−1; ΔHfus=6.02 kJ/mol
Specific heat (dilute aqueous)c ≈ 4.184 J·g−1·K−1

3. Key equations

Ideal gas law
PV = nRT
T in kelvin; R = 0.08206 with P in atm and V in L.
Charles / Gay-Lussac
V/T = const (P,n fixed);   P/T = const (V,n fixed)
Always convert °C → K before ratios.
Dalton's law
Pi = χiPtotal
Mole fractions unchanged by compression if n fixed.
Gas density
d = PM/RT
Plot of d vs 1/T at fixed P has slope PM/R.
Graham's law
rateA/rateB = √(MB/MA)
Lighter gas effuses/diffuses faster.
urms
urms = √(3RT/M)
M in kg/mol when R = 8.314 J·mol−1·K−1.
Henry's law
c = kHPgas
P_gas = χ · P_total for a mixture.
Molarity from mass %
Assume 1.00 L → mass = d×1000 g; mass solute = % × mass; M = (g/MolarMass)
Watch ions: Na3PO4 → 3 Na+.
Calorimetry
q = m c ΔT   or   q = n Cm ΔT
Insulated: Σq ≈ 0. Include phase-change terms (nΔH) when T crosses a transition.
Heat of solution sign
ΔT_solution > 0 ⇒ ΔHsoln < 0 (exo); ΔT < 0 ⇒ ΔHsoln > 0 (endo)
Microscopic: forming ion–dipole is exo; breaking lattice / water H-bonds is endo.

4. Step-by-step procedures

4.1 Ideal-gas temperature or pressure change

  1. Convert every temperature to kelvin.
  2. Identify what is held constant (V rigid → P/T; P balloon → V/T).
  3. Solve the ratio; convert back to °C only at the end if asked.

4.2 Effusion / molar mass from rates

  1. Write Graham: rate₁/rate₂ = √(M₂/M₁).
  2. If one gas is O₂, M_unk = 32 × (rate_O₂/rate_unk)².
  3. Match M to a formula on the choice list.

4.3 Henry's law with air mixtures

  1. Find P_gas = χ · P_total.
  2. c = k_H · P_gas.
  3. Convert M ↔ mM (×1000) to match the answer units.

4.4 Dilution & ion concentrations

  1. mmol of ion = mL × M × (ions per formula).
  2. After mixing, divide by the TOTAL volume.
  3. For a target concentration, V_final = n_ion / C_target.

4.5 Mass % + density → molarity (and [ion])

  1. Work with 1.00 L of solution.
  2. Total grams = density (g/mL) × 1000.
  3. Grams solute = mass-fraction × total grams; moles = g / M.
  4. For [cation], multiply salt molarity by the subscript.

4.6 Metal / water calorimetry

  1. q_water = m_w · 4.184 · (T_f − T_w).
  2. q_metal = −q_water = m_metal · c · (T_f − T_metal).
  3. Solve for c; compare to tabulated metals.

4.7 Ice added to a warm liquid

  1. Warm ice to 0°C (n C_m,ice ΔT).
  2. Melt ice (n ΔH_fus).
  3. Warm resulting water from 0°C to T_f (n C_m,liq ΔT).
  4. That total q came from the other liquid cooling: q = n_liq C_m,liq (T_i − T_f).

4.8 Reading phase diagrams & heating curves

  1. Identify the starting point (solid/liquid/gas).
  2. Lowering P at fixed T often vaporizes a liquid.
  3. On a heating curve: slopes ↔ C_m; plateaus ↔ ΔH_fus or ΔH_vap (usually ΔH_vap is the longer plateau).

4.9 Peptides, φ/ψ, and nucleotide mimics

  1. N-terminus = free amino end (lower residue numbers); C-terminus = free carboxyl.
  2. Most peptide bonds are trans; cis often involves proline.
  3. φ/ψ are backbone single-bond dihedrals that set fold (Ramachandran).
  4. DNA sugars lack 2′-OH; RNA has 2′-OH. Base pairs: A–T (DNA) / A–U (RNA), G–C.

5. Common mistakes

  1. Forgetting °C → K in any gas-law ratio (the most common Exam 3 trap).
  2. Using total pressure instead of partial pressure in Henry's law.
  3. After mixing solutions, dividing by only one volume instead of V₁+V₂ (or the stated final V).
  4. Counting formula units instead of ions (CaCl₂ gives 2 Cl⁻; Na₃PO₄ gives 3 Na⁺).
  5. Treating NH₃ or other weak bases as strong electrolytes.
  6. Thinking melting/vaporization releases heat — both are endothermic; condensation/freezing are exothermic.
  7. Mixing up which microscopic step is exo vs endo when a salt dissolves.
  8. Reading a solubility curve wrong: below = unsaturated, on = saturated, above without ppt = supersaturated.
  9. Assuming φ/ψ describe side-chain rotamers or cis/trans of the peptide bond itself.
  10. Forgetting that lighter gases leave a mixture faster (χ of the heavy gas rises).
  11. Confusing DNA vs RNA nucleotide mimics (look for 2′-OH).
  12. Reporting °C when the algebra was done in K (or the reverse).

6. Units and significant figures

Pressureatm, torr
VolumeL, mL (1000 mL = 1 L)
ConcentrationM = mol/L; mM = mmol/L
Energy / enthalpyJ, kJ; molar heat capacity J·mol−1·K−1; specific heat J·g−1·K−1
TemperatureK for every gas law; ΔT in °C equals ΔT in K
  • Keep extra digits until the end; match the least precise measured value.
  • Exact conversions (1000 mL/L, 273.15) don't cut sig figs.
  • On multiple choice, units and conceptual sign errors matter more than off-by-one in the last digit.

7. Study plan for Exam 3

  1. Take one timed mock Exam 3 (75 min) cold to find weak topics.
  2. Each day: one topic-practice set on your two weakest topics, plus Adaptive review.
  3. Re-work every worked solution you missed on paper without looking.
  4. Day before: a second mock. Night before: re-read sections 3–5 only.
